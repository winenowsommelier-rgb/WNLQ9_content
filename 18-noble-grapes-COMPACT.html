Red Grape Varieties

1. Pinot Noir — The Sensitive Yet Charming Grape

Pinot Noir has thin skin and is extremely sensitive to climate and soil, making it one of the best grapes for expressing terroir. It is the defining grape of Burgundy, France, and also thrives in Oregon, New Zealand, and California's cool coastal regions.

Tasting: Cherry, raspberry, roses, violets. With age: mushroom, truffle, forest floor. Light body, silky tannins.

Regions: Burgundy, Willamette Valley, Central Otago, Sonoma Coast

Quick Stats

Body / TanninsLight / Low–Med
Acidity / AgingHigh / 5–15 yrs
Serve At13–16°C

Western Pairings

Duck breast, grilled salmon, mushroom risotto

Thai Pairings

Grilled chicken, Nam Tok beef salad, Laab Moo

Sommelier Tip

Serve in a large tulip glass. Avoid over-chilling — cold temperatures mute floral complexity.

2. Merlot — Smooth, Approachable, and Loved by All

Named from "Merle" (blackbird), Merlot is smooth, fruit-forward, and the perfect starting point for beginners. On Bordeaux's Right Bank, it dominates estates like Château Pétrus. Often blended with Cabernet Sauvignon for balance.

Tasting: Black cherry, plum, cocoa, chocolate, vanilla. Rounded and velvety.

Regions: Right Bank Bordeaux, Napa Valley, Chile, Washington State

Quick Stats

Body / TanninsMed–Full / Medium
Acidity / AgingMedium / 5–10 yrs
Serve At15–18°C

Western Pairings

Beef pasta, barbecue ribs, roasted lamb

Thai Pairings

Beef Panang, Khao Soi, stir-fried duck with basil

Sommelier Tip

Best value: Chilean Merlot from Colchagua Valley. Ideal gateway wine for beginners.

Explore Merlot

3. Nebbiolo — Italy's Majestic Grape

"Nebbiolo" comes from "nebbia" (fog), referencing the mist of Piedmont's vineyards. It produces Italy's most prestigious wines — Barolo and Barbaresco. Looks deceptively light but packs some of the strongest tannins of any red grape.

Tasting: Dried roses, tar, leather, cherry, truffle. Rich tannins, high acidity.

Regions: Piedmont (Barolo, Barbaresco), Langhe, Roero

Quick Stats

Body / TanninsFull / Very High
Acidity / AgingHigh / 10–30+ yrs
Serve At16–18°C

Western Pairings

Braised beef, mushroom risotto, aged Parmigiano

Thai Pairings

Beef Panang, sun-dried beef, Hang Lay curry

Sommelier Tip

Start with Langhe Nebbiolo. For Barolo, decant at least an hour before serving.

Explore Nebbiolo

4. Cabernet Sauvignon — The King of Red Grapes

The world's most widely planted red grape, born from Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc in 17th-century Bordeaux. Thick skin gives high tannins and deep color, making it naturally suited to oak aging and long cellaring.

Tasting: Blackcurrant, cedar, tobacco, graphite, dark chocolate. Bold tannins soften with age.

Regions: Left Bank Bordeaux, Napa Valley, Coonawarra, Maipo Valley

Quick Stats

Body / TanninsFull / High
Acidity / AgingMed–High / 10–25+ yrs
Serve At16–18°C

Western Pairings

Grilled ribeye, lamb rack, beef Wellington

Thai Pairings

Kor Moo Yang, massaman beef curry, braised oxtail

Sommelier Tip

Decant young Cabernet 30–60 min. Best value: Chilean Cabernet from Maipo Valley.

Explore Cabernet Sauvignon

5. Malbec — Argentina's Bold Ambassador

Originally from Cahors, France, Malbec found its true home in Argentina's Mendoza at 1,000–1,500m elevation. The altitude and intense sunlight create wines with rich fruit, soft tannins, and irresistible appeal.

Tasting: Ripe plum, blackberry, dark chocolate, leather, violet. Generous and juicy.

Regions: Mendoza & Uco Valley (Argentina), Cahors (France)

Quick Stats

Body / TanninsFull / Med–High
Acidity / AgingMedium / 5–12 yrs
Serve At16–18°C

Western Pairings

Grilled steak, empanadas, braised short ribs

Thai Pairings

Seua Rong Hai, Sai Ua sausage, pork red curry

Sommelier Tip

Look for Uco Valley on the label — altitude gives more freshness and complexity.

Explore Malbec

6. Zinfandel — America's Heritage Grape

Genetically identical to Croatia's Crljenak and Italy's Primitivo, Zinfandel became America's signature red in California. Old-vine vineyards produce intensely concentrated wines, often reaching 14–16% alcohol.

Tasting: Blackberry, boysenberry, black pepper, cinnamon, smoky oak. Rich and warming.

Regions: Sonoma, Paso Robles, Lodi (California), Puglia (as Primitivo)

Quick Stats

Body / TanninsFull / Medium
Acidity / AgingMedium / 3–10 yrs
Serve At15–18°C

Western Pairings

BBQ brisket, pepperoni pizza, smoked pork

Thai Pairings

See Krong Moo Yang, garlic pepper pork, Pad Kra Pao

Sommelier Tip

Look for "Old Vine" on labels — low-yielding vines produce more concentrated fruit.

Explore Zinfandel

7. Sangiovese — The Heart of Italian Wines

Italy's most iconic grape — the soul of Tuscany behind Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino, and Super Tuscan blends. The name likely derives from "sanguis Jovis" (blood of Jupiter).

Tasting: Red cherry, cranberry, leather, tobacco, dried herbs. Savory with firm acidity.

Regions: Tuscany (Chianti, Montalcino), Umbria, Emilia-Romagna

Quick Stats

Body / TanninsMed–Full / Med–High
Acidity / AgingHigh / 5–20 yrs
Serve At16–18°C

Western Pairings

Pasta Bolognese, pizza, lasagna

Thai Pairings

Chicken curry, Khao Kluk Kapi, Pad Prik Khing

Sommelier Tip

High acidity makes it the ultimate pasta wine. Brunello di Montalcino: minimum 5 years aging.

Explore Sangiovese

8. Tempranillo — The Spirit of Spanish Wine

The soul of Spanish wine. "Temprano" means "early" because it ripens sooner than other varieties. Spanish winemakers age it in American oak, developing distinctive vanilla and sweet spice aromas.

Tasting: Cherry, blackberry, tobacco, leather, vanilla, oak spice.

Regions: Rioja, Ribera del Duero, Navarra, Toro (Spain)

Quick Stats

Body / TanninsMed–Full / Medium
Acidity / AgingMed–High / 5–20 yrs
Serve At16–18°C

Western Pairings

Grilled steak, tapas, Iberico ham

Thai Pairings

Sai Ua sausage, Kor Moo Yang, Moo Dad Deaw

Sommelier Tip

Rioja Gran Reserva: some of the best value in aged fine wine worldwide.

Explore Tempranillo

9. Syrah (Shiraz) — The Warrior of Red Wines

Same grape, two names: French Syrah is restrained and peppery; Australian Shiraz is bigger and fruit-forward. A key component of the GSM Blend (Grenache-Syrah-Mourvedre).

Tasting: Blackberry, black pepper, black olive, leather, smoky oak. Powerful with a long finish.

Regions: Northern Rhône, Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale, Stellenbosch

Quick Stats

Body / TanninsFull / Med–High
Acidity / AgingMedium / 7–20 yrs
Serve At16–18°C

Western Pairings

Smoked brisket, beef stew, BBQ ribs

Thai Pairings

Hang Lay curry, braised pork belly, duck red curry

Sommelier Tip

Northern Rhône for elegance, Barossa for bold power. Both great with smoky grilled meats.

Explore Syrah/Shiraz

White Grape Varieties

10. Riesling — Refreshing Acidity & Aromatic Elegance

Riesling ranges from bone-dry to intensely sweet, all held together by electric acidity. One of the few whites that ages for decades. Its sweetness and acidity make it perfect for spicy Thai food.

Tasting: Green apple, citrus, honey, white flowers, petrol with age.

Regions: Germany (Mosel, Rheingau), Alsace, Clare Valley (Australia)

Quick Stats

Body / AcidityLight / Very High
Sweetness / AgingDry to Sweet / 10–30 yrs
Serve At8–10°C

Western Pairings

Seafood, roasted pork, foie gras

Thai Pairings

Tom Yum Goong, Som Tam Pla Ra, sour curry

Sommelier Tip

"Trocken" = dry. Off-dry Spätlese is the gold standard for spicy Thai dishes.

Explore Riesling

11. Chenin Blanc — Loire Valley's Versatile Star

The chameleon of white grapes — producing everything from crisp dry wines to dessert wines and sparkling. Heartland is France's Loire Valley; second home in South Africa (called "Steen").

Tasting: Green apple, floral, ginger, honey, citrus.

Regions: Loire Valley (Vouvray), South Africa (Swartland, Stellenbosch)

Quick Stats

Body / AcidityLight–Med / High
Aging / Serve At5–25 yrs / 8–10°C

Western Pairings

Mussels, baked fish, herb-roasted chicken

Thai Pairings

Pla Neung Manao, Gaeng Leung, crab fried rice

Explore Chenin Blanc

12. Gewürztraminer — Unforgettable Aromatics

The most intensely aromatic grape variety in the world. "Gewürz" means "spice" in German. Its combination of sweetness and acidity makes it superb with spicy Asian cuisines.

Tasting: Lychee, rose petals, orange blossom, exotic spices, Turkish delight.

Regions: Alsace (France), Germany, Trentino-Alto Adige (Italy)

Quick Stats

Body / AcidityMed–Full / Low–Med
Aging / Serve At5–15 yrs / 8–10°C

Western Pairings

Foie gras, smoked ham, washed-rind cheese

Thai Pairings

Massaman curry, Tom Kha Gai, Khao Soi

Sommelier Tip

Lychee sweetness cools chili heat beautifully — one of the best wines for spicy food.

Explore Gewürztraminer

13. Viognier — Rhône Valley's Peachy Grape

Nearly extinct by the 1960s (only 30 acres worldwide), Viognier was rescued by passionate winemakers. Today celebrated for its intoxicating floral perfume and rich texture.

Tasting: Jasmine, orange blossom, peach, apricot, ginger. Full-bodied, low acidity.

Regions: Rhône Valley (Condrieu), California, Australia

Quick Stats

Body / AcidityMed–Full / Low–Med
Aging / Serve At3–8 yrs / 10–12°C

Western Pairings

Grilled scallops, lobster bisque, white-sauce pasta

Thai Pairings

Chicken curry, pomelo salad, chicken cashew nut

Explore Viognier

14. Pinot Grigio — Refreshing & Easy-Drinking

The go-to easy-drinking white. Italian style is light and crisp; Alsatian Pinot Gris is richer. Despite being white wine, it has greyish-pink skins ("Gris" means grey).

Tasting: Fresh pear, apple, lemon, white flowers, mineral notes.

Regions: Italy (Veneto, Friuli), Alsace, Germany, Oregon

Quick Stats

Body / AcidityLight–Med / Med–High
Aging / Serve At2–5 yrs / 7–9°C

Western Pairings

Sashimi, fresh oysters, light pasta

Thai Pairings

Yum Woon Sen, seafood Tom Yum, seaweed salad

Explore Pinot Grigio

15. Sauvignon Blanc — Crisp & Herbaceous

The world's most refreshing white grape. Parent grape of Cabernet Sauvignon. Achieved superstar status in New Zealand's Marlborough and is a key component of Sauternes sweet wines.

Tasting: Fresh-cut grass, green herbs, passionfruit, grapefruit. Electrifying acidity.

Regions: Sancerre (Loire), Marlborough (NZ), Bordeaux, Chile

Quick Stats

Body / AcidityLight–Med / High
Aging / Serve At2–7 yrs / 8–10°C

Western Pairings

Fresh oysters, steamed fish, goat cheese salad

Thai Pairings

Green mango salad, Gaeng Liang, fish Tom Yum

Sommelier Tip

Marlborough for vibrant fruit, Sancerre for subtle elegance. Same grape, two personalities.

Explore Sauvignon Blanc

16. Moscato — Aromatic, Sweet & Charming

The ultimate beginner-friendly wine — sweet, gently fizzy, low alcohol (5–7%). Most famous as Italy's Moscato d'Asti. The Muscat family is one of the oldest cultivated grape varieties.

Tasting: Peach, lychee, orange blossom, apricot, honey. Gently sweet with effervescence.

Regions: Piedmont (Italy), Spain, Southern France, California

Quick Stats

Body / SweetnessLight / Sweet
Aging / Serve At1–3 yrs / 6–8°C

Western Pairings

Fruit cake, macarons, Brie with honey

Thai Pairings

Mango sticky rice, Tub Tim Krob, crispy roti

Sommelier Tip

Only 5–7% alcohol — perfect for guests who find regular wine too strong. Pairs brilliantly with Thai desserts.

Explore Moscato

17. Semillon — Bordeaux's Legendary Sweet Wine Grape

Semillon leads a double life: primary grape in Sauternes (world's most famous sweet wine via noble rot), and in Australia's Hunter Valley it makes bone-dry whites that age for decades.

Tasting: Green apple, honey, pear, lemon, toast. Sweet styles: dried apricot, saffron.

Regions: Bordeaux (Sauternes), Hunter Valley (Australia), South Africa

Quick Stats

Body / AcidityMed–Full / Med–High
Aging / Serve At10–30+ yrs / 8–10°C

Western Pairings

Foie gras, blue cheese, crème brûlée

Thai Pairings

Coconut desserts, bananas in coconut milk

Explore Semillon

18. Chardonnay — The Queen of White Grapes

The world's most widely planted white grape. No other white adapts so completely to its environment — from steely Chablis to buttery Napa Valley. Also a key ingredient in Champagne (Blanc de Blancs = 100% Chardonnay).

Tasting: Cool climate: green apple, lemon, mineral. Warm: pineapple, mango, butter, vanilla, toast.

Regions: Burgundy (Chablis, Meursault), Napa & Sonoma, Margaret River, Champagne

Quick Stats

Body / AcidityMed–Full / Med–High
Aging / Serve At5–20+ yrs / 10–14°C

Western Pairings

Lobster, roast chicken, creamy risotto

Thai Pairings

Poo Phad Pong Karee, green curry, grilled seafood

Sommelier Tip

Chablis at 8–10°C for crisp freshness. Oaked Chardonnay at 12–14°C for creamy texture.

Explore Chardonnay

FAQ: Wine Grapes

What are the "noble grapes"?

Originally 6 varieties (Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Riesling) that produce world-class wine anywhere. This expanded list covers 18 globally recognized varieties.

Which grape is best for beginners?

Red: Merlot (smooth). White: Moscato (sweet, low alcohol) or Pinot Grigio (crisp, easy).

Best wine grapes for Thai food?

Off-dry Riesling for spicy dishes, Gewürztraminer for coconut curries, Sauvignon Blanc for Thai salads and seafood.

Syrah vs Shiraz?

Same grape. "Syrah" in France (elegant, peppery), "Shiraz" in Australia (riper, bolder).

Which grapes age longest?

Reds: Nebbiolo 30+ yrs, Cabernet 25+ yrs. Whites: Riesling 30+ yrs, Semillon (Sauternes) 50+ yrs.

Wine serving temperatures?

Light reds 13–16°C, full reds 16–18°C, light whites 7–10°C, full whites 10–14°C, sweet/Moscato 6–8°C.
