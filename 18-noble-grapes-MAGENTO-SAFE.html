Red Grape Varieties

Red wine grapes produce the world's most age-worthy and food-friendly wines. From the silky transparency of Pinot Noir to the tannic power of Cabernet Sauvignon, these nine varieties are responsible for the vast majority of fine red wine produced globally.

1. Pinot Noir — The Sensitive Yet Charming Grape

Pinot Noir: thin-skinned, terroir-driven, and endlessly elegant

Often dubbed "the temperamental grape," Pinot Noir has thin skin and is extremely sensitive to climate and soil. This makes it one of the best grapes for expressing terroir — the unique character of where it grows. It is the defining grape of Burgundy, France, producing some of the most sought-after wines on Earth.

The word "Pinot" derives from "pine" because the grape clusters resemble pine cones. Beyond Burgundy, Pinot Noir thrives in Oregon's Willamette Valley, New Zealand's Central Otago, and California's cooler coastal regions.

Tasting Profile: Cherry, raspberry, roses, violets, subtle spice. With age, earthy mushroom, truffle, and forest floor. Light body with silky tannins.

Key Regions: Burgundy (France), Willamette Valley (Oregon), Central Otago (New Zealand), Sonoma Coast & Santa Barbara (California)

Quick Stats

AcidityHigh
BodyLight
TanninsLow–Medium
Aging5–15 years
Serve At13–16°C
GlassLarge tulip

Western Pairings

Duck breast with cherry sauce, grilled salmon, mushroom risotto, roasted quail

Thai Pairings

Grilled crispy-skinned chicken, spicy beef salad (Nam Tok), spicy minced pork salad (Laab Moo)

Sommelier Tip

Serve at 13–16°C in a large tulip-shaped glass to maximize delicate aromas. Avoid over-chilling — cold temperatures mute the floral and fruit complexity.

2. Merlot — Smooth, Approachable, and Loved by All

Merlot: the approachable everyday wine with serious depth

Merlot takes its name from "Merle," meaning "blackbird" — a nod to the birds that love these sweet, plump grapes. Smooth, fruit-forward, and easy-drinking, Merlot is one of the world's most popular reds and a perfect starting point for wine beginners.

On Bordeaux's Right Bank, Merlot dominates legendary estates like Château Pétrus and the Grand Crus of Saint-Émilion. It is frequently blended with Cabernet Sauvignon — Merlot adds softness and plummy fruit while Cabernet adds tannin backbone.

Tasting Profile: Black cherry, plum, cocoa, chocolate, coffee, vanilla. Rounded and velvety with moderate tannins.

Key Regions: Right Bank Bordeaux (Saint-Émilion, Pomerol), Napa Valley (California), Chile, Washington State

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium
BodyMedium–Full
TanninsMedium
Aging5–10 years
Serve At15–18°C
GlassStandard red

Western Pairings

Beef pasta with rich sauce, meat pies, barbecue ribs, roasted lamb

Thai Pairings

Beef Panang curry, chicken Khao Soi, stir-fried duck with basil

Sommelier Tip

Merlot is the ideal gateway wine for beginners. For the best value, look to Chilean Merlot from the Colchagua Valley.

3. Nebbiolo — Italy's Majestic Grape

Nebbiolo: the fog-shrouded grape behind Italy's greatest wines

"Nebbiolo" comes from "nebbia," Italian for "fog," referencing the mist that blankets Piedmont's vineyards during harvest. This grape produces Italy's most prestigious wines — Barolo and Barbaresco — often called the "King and Queen of Italian Wines."

Nebbiolo looks deceptively light in the glass but packs some of the strongest tannins of any red grape. Properly aged Barolo can rival the finest Burgundy in complexity.

Tasting Profile: Dried roses, tar, leather, licorice, baked earth, cherry, truffle. Rich tannins with high acidity.

Key Regions: Piedmont (Barolo, Barbaresco), Langhe, Roero — almost exclusively Italy

Quick Stats

AcidityHigh
BodyFull
TanninsVery High
Aging10–30+ years
Serve At16–18°C
GlassLarge Burgundy

Western Pairings

Braised beef, porcini mushroom risotto, wine-braised duck, aged Parmigiano

Thai Pairings

Beef Panang, deep-fried sun-dried beef, Hang Lay pork curry

Sommelier Tip

Start with Langhe Nebbiolo — more approachable and affordable than Barolo. When ready for the real thing, decant Barolo for at least an hour.

4. Cabernet Sauvignon — The King of Red Grapes

Cabernet Sauvignon: the undisputed king of red wine grapes worldwide

Cabernet Sauvignon is the world's most widely planted red grape. Born from a natural cross between Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc in 17th-century Bordeaux, it produces powerful, structured wines with intense dark fruit and remarkable aging potential.

Its thick skin gives high tannin levels and deep color, making it naturally suited to oak aging. From Bordeaux's Left Bank châteaux to the cult wines of Napa Valley, Cabernet is the benchmark for serious red wine.

Tasting Profile: Blackcurrant (cassis), black cherry, cedar, tobacco, graphite, dark chocolate, eucalyptus. Bold tannins soften beautifully with age.

Key Regions: Left Bank Bordeaux (France), Napa Valley (California), Coonawarra (Australia), Maipo Valley (Chile)

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium–High
BodyFull
TanninsHigh
Aging10–25+ years
Serve At16–18°C
GlassBordeaux glass

Western Pairings

Grilled ribeye steak, lamb rack with rosemary, beef Wellington, aged cheddar

Thai Pairings

Grilled pork neck (Kor Moo Yang), massaman beef curry, braised oxtail soup

Sommelier Tip

Young Cabernet benefits from decanting 30–60 minutes before serving. For everyday value, Chilean Cabernet from Maipo Valley is outstanding.

5. Malbec — Argentina's Bold Ambassador

Malbec: from Cahors to Mendoza — a grape reborn in the Andes

Malbec's story is a great comeback tale. Originally from Cahors in southwest France, it struggled there and was nearly abandoned. Then Argentine winemakers in Mendoza discovered it thrived at high altitudes, producing wines with richer fruit, softer tannins, and irresistible appeal.

The best examples come from Uco Valley vineyards at 1,000–1,500 meters elevation, where intense sunlight and cool nights create concentrated, balanced wines.

Tasting Profile: Ripe plum, blackberry, blueberry, dark chocolate, leather, sweet tobacco, violet. Generous and juicy with velvety tannins.

Key Regions: Mendoza & Uco Valley (Argentina), Cahors (France), Salta (Argentina)

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium
BodyFull
TanninsMedium–High
Aging5–12 years
Serve At16–18°C
GlassStandard red

Western Pairings

Argentine asado (grilled steak), empanadas, braised short ribs, blue cheese burgers

Thai Pairings

Crying tiger grilled beef (Seua Rong Hai), Northern Thai sausage (Sai Ua), pork red curry

Sommelier Tip

For the best value in bold red wine, Argentine Malbec is hard to beat. Look for Uco Valley bottles — the altitude gives more freshness and complexity.

6. Zinfandel — America's Heritage Grape

Zinfandel: California's own — bold, jammy, and full of personality

DNA testing revealed Zinfandel is genetically identical to Croatia's Crljenak Kaštelanski and Italy's Primitivo. But it was in California that Zinfandel found its identity, becoming America's signature red grape.

Old-vine vineyards — some over 100 years old — produce intensely concentrated, high-alcohol wines. Serious Zinfandel is a bold, spicy red with personality to spare, often reaching 14–16% alcohol.

Tasting Profile: Ripe blackberry, boysenberry, black pepper, cinnamon, clove, dried fruit, smoky oak. Rich and warming.

Key Regions: Sonoma County & Paso Robles (California), Puglia (Italy, as Primitivo), Lodi (California)

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium
BodyFull
TanninsMedium
Aging3–10 years
Serve At15–18°C
GlassStandard red

Western Pairings

BBQ brisket, pepperoni pizza, spicy sausage pasta, smoked pulled pork

Thai Pairings

Grilled pork ribs (See Krong Moo Yang), garlic pepper pork, spicy basil minced pork (Pad Kra Pao)

Sommelier Tip

Look for "Old Vine" on the label — these wines come from low-yielding vines that produce more concentrated fruit. Zinfandel is a natural match for bold, grilled meats.

7. Sangiovese — The Heart of Italian Wines

Sangiovese: the beating heart of Tuscan winemaking

Sangiovese is Italy's most iconic grape — the soul of Tuscany. It is the primary variety behind Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino, and Super Tuscan blends. The name likely derives from "sanguis Jovis," meaning "the blood of Jupiter."

Tasting Profile: Red cherry, cranberry, violet, leather, tobacco, dried herbs, earth. Savory and structured with firm acidity.

Key Regions: Tuscany (Chianti, Montalcino, Montepulciano), Umbria, Emilia-Romagna

Quick Stats

AcidityHigh
BodyMedium–Full
TanninsMedium–High
Aging5–20 years
Serve At16–18°C
GlassStandard red

Western Pairings

Pasta Bolognese, pepperoni pizza, lasagna, grilled Italian sausage

Thai Pairings

Chicken curry, shrimp paste fried rice (Khao Kluk Kapi), pork with chili paste (Pad Prik Khing)

Sommelier Tip

Sangiovese's high acidity makes it the ultimate pasta wine. For complexity, select Brunello di Montalcino — minimum 5 years aging by law.

8. Tempranillo — The Spirit of Spanish Wine

Tempranillo: the early-ripening grape behind Spain's greatest reds

Tempranillo is the soul of Spanish wine. Its name comes from "temprano," meaning "early," because it ripens earlier than other varieties. Spanish winemakers age it in American oak, developing distinctive vanilla and sweet spice aromas.

Tasting Profile: Cherry, blackberry, tobacco leaf, leather, vanilla, oak spice. Well-structured with balanced tannins.

Key Regions: Rioja, Ribera del Duero, Navarra, Toro (Spain)

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium–High
BodyMedium–Full
TanninsMedium
Aging5–20 years
Serve At16–18°C
GlassStandard red

Western Pairings

Char-grilled steak, Spanish tapas, Iberico ham, roasted lamb

Thai Pairings

Northern Thai sausage (Sai Ua), grilled pork neck (Kor Moo Yang), sun-dried pork (Moo Dad Deaw)

Sommelier Tip

Choose "Reserva" or "Gran Reserva" labels for complex vanilla-and-leather character from extended oak aging. Rioja Gran Reserva offers some of the best value in aged fine wine.

9. Syrah (Shiraz) — The Warrior of Red Wines

Syrah in France, Shiraz in Australia — same grape, two distinct personalities

Syrah and Shiraz are the same grape. French Syrah is more restrained and peppery, while Australian Shiraz is bigger, riper, and more fruit-forward. It is a key component of the famous GSM Blend (Grenache-Syrah-Mourvedre).

Tasting Profile: Blackberry, blueberry, black pepper, black olive, leather, smoky oak, dark chocolate. Powerful with a long finish.

Key Regions: Northern Rhône Valley (France), Barossa Valley & McLaren Vale (Australia), Stellenbosch (South Africa)

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium
BodyFull
TanninsMedium–High
Aging7–20 years
Serve At16–18°C
GlassStandard red

Western Pairings

Smoked beef brisket, beef stew, BBQ pork ribs, game meats

Thai Pairings

Hang Lay pork curry, braised pork belly, red curry with roasted duck

Sommelier Tip

Choose Syrah from Northern Rhône for elegance or Shiraz from Barossa Valley for bold, fruit-driven power. Both pair brilliantly with smoky, grilled meats.

White Grape Varieties

White wine grapes offer an astonishing range — from bone-dry and mineral to lushly sweet and aromatic. These nine represent the full spectrum, from Riesling's laser-focused acidity to Chardonnay's tropical richness.

10. Riesling — Refreshing Acidity & Aromatic Elegance

Riesling: Germany's queen of white wines — from bone-dry to lusciously sweet

Riesling produces wines ranging from bone-dry to intensely sweet, all held together by electric acidity. It is one of the few whites capable of aging for decades. What makes Riesling perfect for Thai food lovers is its natural affinity for spicy cuisine — the sweetness and acidity counterbalance chili heat.

Tasting Profile: Green apple, citrus, honey, white flowers, and a distinctive petrol aroma with age.

Key Regions: Germany (Mosel, Rheingau), France (Alsace), Australia (Clare Valley, Eden Valley)

Quick Stats

AcidityVery High
BodyLight
SweetnessDry to Sweet
Aging10–30 years
Serve At8–10°C

Western Pairings

Seafood, apple-roasted pork, foie gras (with sweet Riesling)

Thai Pairings

Tom Yum Goong, fermented fish papaya salad (Som Tam Pla Ra), Southern Thai sour curry

Sommelier Tip

Choose dry Riesling (look for "Trocken" on German labels) for a crisp aperitif, or off-dry Spätlese to pair with spicy Thai dishes.

11. Chenin Blanc — Loire Valley's Versatile Star

Chenin Blanc: one grape, endless styles — from sparkling to sweet

Chenin Blanc is the chameleon of white grapes — producing everything from crisp dry wines to luscious dessert wines and sparkling. Its heartland is France's Loire Valley, but it has found a second home in South Africa, where it is called "Steen."

Tasting Profile: Green apple, floral notes, ginger, honey, citrus. Styles range from refreshingly crisp to luxuriously sweet.

Key Regions: Loire Valley (Vouvray, Savennieres), South Africa (Swartland, Stellenbosch)

Quick Stats

AcidityHigh
BodyLight–Medium
Aging5–25 years
Serve At8–10°C

Western Pairings

Mussels steamed in white wine, baked fish with butter sauce, herb-roasted chicken

Thai Pairings

Steamed fish with lime (Pla Neung Manao), yellow curry (Gaeng Leung), crab fried rice

Sommelier Tip

Chenin Blanc's refreshing acidity and complex aromatics make it one of the best all-rounder whites for any meal.

12. Gewürztraminer — An Unforgettable Aromatic Experience

Gewürztraminer: the most intensely aromatic white grape in the world

Gewürztraminer is the most intensely aromatic grape variety in the world. The name comes from "Gewürz," German for "spice." One sniff and you will never forget it. Its combination of sweetness and acidity makes it superb with spicy Asian cuisines.

Tasting Profile: Lychee, rose petals, orange blossom, exotic spices, Turkish delight. Richly textured with a pleasantly bitter finish.

Key Regions: Alsace (France), Germany, Northern Italy (Trentino-Alto Adige)

Quick Stats

AcidityLow–Medium
BodyMedium–Full
Aging5–15 years
Serve At8–10°C

Western Pairings

Foie gras, smoked ham, soft washed-rind cheeses

Thai Pairings

Massaman curry, Tom Kha Gai (coconut chicken soup), Khao Soi, salt-and-pepper shrimp

Sommelier Tip

Serve lightly chilled at 8–10°C. The lychee sweetness cools chili heat beautifully — one of the best wines for spicy food.

13. Viognier — Rhône Valley's Peachy Aromatic Grape

Viognier: nearly extinct a generation ago, now a global favorite

Viognier nearly disappeared — by the 1960s, only about 30 acres remained worldwide. Passionate winemakers in France and California brought it back, and today Viognier is celebrated for its intoxicating floral perfume and rich texture.

Tasting Profile: Jasmine, orange blossom, peach, apricot, ginger, citrus peel. Full-bodied with low acidity.

Key Regions: Rhône Valley (Condrieu — France), California, Australia

Quick Stats

AcidityLow–Medium
BodyMedium–Full
Aging3–8 years
Serve At10–12°C

Western Pairings

Grilled scallops, cheese soufflé, white-sauce pasta, lobster bisque

Thai Pairings

Chicken curry, pomelo salad, glass noodles with shrimp, chicken with cashews

Sommelier Tip

Enjoy Viognier young — unlike Riesling, it does not improve with age. Perfect if you love aromatic whites but find high-acid wines too sharp.

14. Pinot Grigio — Refreshing & Easy-Drinking White Wine

Pinot Grigio: crisp, clean, and effortlessly refreshing

Pinot Grigio (Pinot Gris in France) is the go-to easy-drinking white. Italian style is light and crisp; Alsatian Pinot Gris is richer and sometimes off-dry. Despite being a white wine, it has greyish-pink skins ("Gris" means grey).

Tasting Profile: Fresh pear, apple, lemon, white flowers, sometimes mineral or almond notes.

Key Regions: Italy (Veneto, Friuli), France (Alsace), Germany, Oregon (USA)

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium–High
BodyLight–Medium
Aging2–5 years
Serve At7–9°C

Western Pairings

Seafood sashimi, fruit salads, fresh oysters, light pasta

Thai Pairings

Glass noodle salad (Yum Woon Sen), salted shrimp, seaweed salad, clear seafood Tom Yum

Sommelier Tip

Serve well-chilled at 7–9°C. Drink within 2 years of vintage for maximum freshness — not a wine for cellaring.

15. Sauvignon Blanc — Crisp, Refreshing & Herbaceous

Sauvignon Blanc: the electric, green-tinged white that wakes up your palate

Sauvignon Blanc is the world's most refreshing white grape. Originating in Loire Valley, it achieved superstar status in New Zealand's Marlborough region. It is also a parent grape of Cabernet Sauvignon and a key component of Sauternes sweet wines.

Tasting Profile: Fresh-cut grass, green herbs, tomato leaf, passionfruit, grapefruit, citrus. Electrifying acidity.

Key Regions: Loire Valley (Sancerre), Marlborough (New Zealand), Bordeaux (France), Chile

Quick Stats

AcidityHigh
BodyLight–Medium
Aging2–7 years
Serve At8–10°C

Western Pairings

Fresh oysters, steamed seabass with lemon, goat cheese salad

Thai Pairings

Green mango salad, Gaeng Liang (vegetable soup), clear Tom Yum fish, fish rice porridge

Sommelier Tip

Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc is the benchmark for crisp whites. For something more subtle, try Sancerre — same grape, completely different personality.

16. Moscato — Aromatic, Sweet & Charming

Moscato: the world's most approachable wine — sweet, fizzy, and irresistible

Moscato is the ultimate beginner-friendly wine — sweet, gently fizzy, low in alcohol (5–7%), and bursting with fruity aromas. Its most famous expression is Italy's Moscato d'Asti. The Muscat family is one of the oldest cultivated grape varieties.

Tasting Profile: Peach, lychee, orange blossom, apricot, honey, white flowers. Gently sweet with delicate effervescence.

Key Regions: Piedmont (Italy), Spain, Southern France, California (USA)

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium
BodyLight
SweetnessSweet
Aging1–3 years
Serve At6–8°C

Western Pairings

Fruit cake, macarons, Brie cheese with honey, fresh fruit tarts

Thai Pairings

Mango sticky rice, crispy ruby dessert (Tub Tim Krob), crispy roti, fresh fruits

Sommelier Tip

Serve well-chilled (6–8°C). With only 5–7% alcohol, it is perfect for guests who find regular wine too strong — and pairs brilliantly with Thai desserts.

17. Semillon — Heart of Bordeaux's Legendary Sweet Wines

Semillon: the golden grape behind Sauternes — and a dry wine secret in Australia

Semillon leads a double life. In Bordeaux, it is the primary grape in Sauternes — arguably the world's most famous sweet wine, made through noble rot (Botrytis). In Australia's Hunter Valley, it makes bone-dry whites that develop remarkable complexity over decades.

Tasting Profile: Green apple, honey, pear, lemon, toast, minerals. Sweet styles add dried apricot, saffron, marmalade.

Key Regions: Bordeaux (France), Hunter Valley (Australia), South Africa

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium–High
BodyMedium–Full
Aging10–30+ years
Serve At8–10°C

Western Pairings

Foie gras, fruit desserts, blue cheese, crème brûlée (with Sauternes)

Thai Pairings

Thai coconut desserts, bananas in sweet coconut milk, rich coconut curries (with Sauternes)

Sommelier Tip

A half-bottle of Sauternes with Thai coconut desserts is a pairing you will never forget. Sweet styles develop astonishing complexity over decades.

18. Chardonnay — The Queen of White Grapes

Chardonnay: the blank canvas that reflects its terroir like no other

Chardonnay is the world's most widely planted white grape. No other white adapts so completely to its environment — from steely Chablis to buttery Napa Valley. It is also a key ingredient in Champagne (Blanc de Blancs is 100% Chardonnay).

Tasting Profile: Cool climates: green apple, lemon, mineral. Warm climates: pineapple, mango, peach, butter, vanilla, toast from oak aging.

Key Regions: Burgundy (Chablis, Meursault, Montrachet), Napa & Sonoma (California), Margaret River (Australia), Champagne

Quick Stats

AcidityMedium–High
BodyMedium–Full
Aging5–20+ years
Serve At10–14°C

Western Pairings

Lobster with butter sauce, roast chicken, creamy risotto, grilled salmon

Thai Pairings

Crab curry (Poo Phad Pong Karee), chicken green curry, grilled seafood with garlic butter

Sommelier Tip

Serve unoaked Chardonnay (Chablis) at 8–10°C for crisp freshness. Rich, oaked Chardonnay at 12–14°C to showcase creamy texture.

Frequently Asked Questions About Wine Grapes

What are the "noble grapes" and why are they called that?

The term "noble grapes" traditionally refers to grape varieties considered the finest in international winemaking. The original six — Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Pinot Noir (reds) and Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Riesling (whites) — could produce world-class wine regardless of where they were grown. This expanded list of 18 includes additional varieties that have earned global recognition.

Which wine grape is best for beginners?

For red wine, start with Merlot — smooth and approachable. For white, try Moscato (sweet, low alcohol) or Pinot Grigio (crisp, easy-drinking). As your palate develops, try Pinot Noir (red) and Sauvignon Blanc (white).

What wine grapes pair best with Thai food?

Off-dry Riesling is the gold standard for spicy Thai dishes — sweetness tempers chili heat while acidity matches the food. Gewürztraminer works with coconut curries, and Sauvignon Blanc is excellent with Thai salads and seafood.

What is the difference between Syrah and Shiraz?

Same grape, different names. "Syrah" is used in France and Europe; "Shiraz" in Australia and the New World. French Syrah tends to be more elegant and peppery; Australian Shiraz is typically riper, fruitier, and more powerful.

Which wine grapes age the longest?

Reds: Nebbiolo (Barolo) 30+ years, Cabernet Sauvignon 25+ years, Tempranillo (Gran Reserva) 20+ years. Whites: Riesling 30+ years, Semillon (Sauternes) 50+ years, Chenin Blanc 25+ years.

What is the most popular red wine grape in the world?

Cabernet Sauvignon is the world's most widely planted red grape, grown in virtually every major wine-producing country. Merlot is a close second.

What temperature should I serve wine at?

Light reds (Pinot Noir): 13–16°C. Full reds (Cabernet, Syrah): 16–18°C. Light whites (Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc): 7–10°C. Full whites (oaked Chardonnay): 10–14°C. Sweet wines & Moscato: 6–8°C.
