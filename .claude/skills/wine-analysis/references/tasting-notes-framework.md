# Wine Tasting Notes Framework

## Standard Tasting Notes Structure

Professional tasting notes follow a structured approach:

### 1. Appearance (Visual Assessment)

**Color Categories**:
- **Red Wines**: Ruby, garnet, brick, tawny
- **White Wines**: Pale yellow, golden, amber, brown
- **Rosé Wines**: Pink, salmon, onion skin

**Clarity & Intensity**:
- Clarity: Clear, slightly hazy, cloudy
- Intensity: Pale, medium, deep, opaque
- Condition: Fresh appearance, oxidized appearance

**Example**: "Deep ruby with garnet edges, indicating maturity"

### 2. Aroma/Bouquet (Olfactory Assessment)

**Primary Aromas** (from grape variety):
- Floral: Rose, violet, jasmine, honeysuckle
- Fruity: Black cherry, raspberry, plum, citrus, apple
- Herbal: Grass, green pepper, mint, eucalyptus
- Spicy: Pepper, clove, anise, cinnamon

**Secondary Aromas** (from fermentation):
- Yeasty, bready, buttery, creamy
- Vanilla, caramel, butterscotch

**Tertiary Aromas** (from aging):
- Oak: Vanilla, toast, spice, cedar
- Development: Leather, tobacco, coffee, chocolate
- Evolution: Earthy, animal, mushroom notes

**Assessment Approach**:
1. Take initial nose on first opening
2. Swirl glass to aerate
3. Take second, deeper nose
4. Note how aromas evolve

**Example**: "Nose displays ripe blackberry, plum, with hints of oak spice and graphite minerality"

### 3. Palate (Taste Assessment)

**Flavor Profile**:
- Initial flavors on entry
- Mid-palate development
- Finish characteristics

**Structure Elements**:
- **Acidity**: Crisp, balanced, flabby
- **Tannins** (reds): Fine, silky, firm, astringent, resolved
- **Alcohol**: Balanced, warming, hot
- **Body**: Light, medium, full
- **Sweetness**: Bone-dry, dry, off-dry, sweet

**Mouthfeel**:
- Texture: Smooth, velvety, grippy, juicy
- Weight: Light, medium, full
- Finish length: Short, medium, long

**Example**: "Palate shows dark cherry and plum with structured tannins, bright acidity, and a long, spiced finish"

### 4. Overall Assessment

**Vintage & Aging**:
- Drinking window: Ready to drink now vs. cellar potential
- Age-ability factors: Acidity, tannin structure, concentration

**Food Pairing Suggestions**:
- Main dish pairings
- Alternative pairings for flexibility
- Cheese pairings if applicable

**Value Assessment**:
- Quality-to-price relationship
- Competitive positioning in category

**Example**: "This wine is drinking beautifully now with 5-10 years of aging potential. Excellent value in the category."

## Common Wine Descriptors by Category

### Red Wine Descriptors

**Fruity Notes**:
- Black fruit: Blackberry, black currant, plum, black cherry
- Red fruit: Strawberry, raspberry, red cherry, cranberry
- Stone fruit: Peach, apricot (in lighter reds)

**Spice & Oak**:
- Spice: Black pepper, white pepper, licorice, star anise
- Oak: Vanilla, toast, cedar, smoke, char
- Herbal: Leather, tobacco, earth, forest floor

**Full-Bodied Red Examples**:
- "Cabernet Sauvignon: Blackberry, black currant, cedar, tobacco, structured tannins"
- "Syrah: Dark plum, black pepper, leather, earthy minerality"
- "Nebbiolo: Cherry, rose, tar, leather, powerful tannins"

**Medium-Bodied Red Examples**:
- "Pinot Noir: Red cherry, raspberry, earthy mushroom, silky tannins"
- "Grenache: Red fruit, white pepper, spice, juicy acidity"

### White Wine Descriptors

**Fruity Notes**:
- Tree fruit: Apple, pear, peach, apricot
- Citrus: Lemon, lime, grapefruit, orange
- Tropical: Mango, pineapple, passion fruit, guava

**Mineral & Floral**:
- Mineral: Chalky, flinty, saline, steely
- Floral: Honeysuckle, white flowers, acacia, orange blossom
- Herbal: Grass, hay, green tea, herbs

**Full-Bodied White Examples**:
- "Chardonnay (oak): Apple, citrus, toast, butter, creamy texture"
- "Viognier: Apricot, peach, white flowers, full body"

**Crisp White Examples**:
- "Sauvignon Blanc: Citrus, grass, green apple, bright acidity"
- "Riesling: Apple, honey, floral, minerality, slight sweetness"

### Rosé Wine Descriptors
- "Pink grapefruit, strawberry, citrus, crisp acidity"
- "Red fruit, herbs, light body, refreshing"

## Tasting Note Quality Standards

### What Makes Quality Tasting Notes

✅ **Good Tasting Notes**:
- Specific descriptors (not just "fruity")
- Clear structure following appearance → aroma → palate → conclusion
- Balanced observations (positive and areas for development)
- Accurate to wine type and vintage
- Accessible language (explains technical terms)
- Actionable pairing suggestions
- Appropriate context (vintage, producer, region)

❌ **Poor Tasting Notes**:
- Vague descriptors: "Nice", "good", "complex" (without specifics)
- Inaccurate notes: Expecting tropical fruit in Pinot Noir
- Gatekeeping language: Overly technical without explanation
- Missing structure: Jumbled observations
- Unbalanced: Only positive or only negative
- Inconsistent: Contradictory observations

### Credibility Checklist

When evaluating sommelier tasting notes:

1. **Accuracy Check**: Do descriptors match typical profile for variety/region?
2. **Specificity Check**: Are observations concrete vs. vague adjectives?
3. **Balance Check**: Does note include both strengths and development areas?
4. **Accessibility Check**: Could an enthusiast understand the note?
5. **Consistency Check**: Are observations logically connected?
6. **Source Check**: Is the tasting note grounded in wine science?

## Regional Tasting Note Conventions

### Burgundy Wines
- Focus on terroir expression
- Elegant language: "silky", "refined", "elegant"
- Emphasis on mineral characteristics
- Detailed vintage variation discussion

### Bordeaux Wines
- Structure-focused: Tannin, acidity, aging potential
- Power/elegance language: "powerful", "complex", "structured"
- Left bank vs. right bank characteristics
- Blend components mentioned

### Rhône Wines
- Fruit-forward language for southern Rhône
- "Ripe", "juicy", "spiced" common descriptors
- Northern vs. southern distinction important

### New World Wines
- Often more fruit-forward descriptors
- "Opulent", "generous", "modern" language common
- Alcohol level often mentioned
- Production methods may be discussed

## Common Tasting Note Mistakes

### Mistake #1: Projecting Expected Notes
- ❌ Expecting blackberry in light Pinot Noir
- ✅ Tasting what's actually there (red fruit, minerals)

### Mistake #2: Overusing Flowery Language
- ❌ "This wine is a symphony of elegance"
- ✅ "This wine shows red cherry, herbs, and balanced acidity"

### Mistake #3: Not Understanding Vintage Variation
- ❌ Describing all Bordeaux identically
- ✅ Acknowledging how weather affected that vintage

### Mistake #4: Missing the Finish
- ❌ Tasting only the initial impression
- ✅ Noting how flavors develop and linger

### Mistake #5: Ignoring Food Context
- ❌ Tasting wine in isolation
- ✅ Considering how wine will perform with food

## Developing Tasting Note Skills

### Practice Progression

1. **Beginner**: Appearance, basic fruit notes, acid/tannin/alcohol
2. **Intermediate**: More specific descriptors, structure analysis, aging potential
3. **Advanced**: Subtle nuances, terroir expression, vintage characteristics
4. **Expert**: Complex aging scenarios, production method detection, rare comparisons

### Sensory Training Exercises

1. **Fruit Blind Tasting**: Identify grape varieties without knowing
2. **Regional Comparison**: Taste wines from same variety, different regions
3. **Vintage Vertical**: Taste same wine across multiple years
4. **Pairing Practice**: Taste wine before, during, after food pairings
5. **Quality Comparison**: Compare budget vs. premium within category
