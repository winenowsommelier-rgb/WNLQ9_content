# Wine Pairing Principles & Framework

## Core Pairing Principles

### 1. Complementary Pairing (Match & Enhance)

**Concept**: Wine and food share similar flavor characteristics that enhance each other.

**Examples**:
- Earthy red wine (Pinot Noir) + earthy mushroom dishes
- Herbaceous wine (Sauvignon Blanc) + herb-forward cuisines
- Fruity wine (Riesling) + fruit-based sauces

**Why It Works**: Shared flavor bridges create harmony and intensity.

### 2. Contrasting Pairing (Cut Through & Balance)

**Concept**: Wine characteristics contrast with food to balance or cut through richness.

**Examples**:
- Acidic wine (Vermentino) + rich, fatty dishes (butter, cream, oil)
- Tannic wine (Cabernet) + high-protein, fat dishes (steak, pâté)
- Sweet wine (Riesling) + spicy food (cuts heat, adds sweetness)
- Dry wine (Champagne) + salty appetizers (balances salt)

**Why It Works**: Opposite characteristics neutralize extremes and cleanse palate.

### 3. Weight Matching

**Concept**: Match wine body and intensity to dish richness and intensity.

**Weight Scale**:

| Wine Weight | Typical Wines | Food Pairing |
|------------|---------------|--------------|
| Light | Albariño, Pinot Grigio, Beaujolais | Light appetizers, salads, fish |
| Medium | Chardonnay, Grenache, Barbera | Moderate proteins, pasta, poultry |
| Full | Cabernet, Burgundy reds, Amarone | Rich meats, game, heavy sauces |

**Why It Works**: Balanced structure prevents wine from being overwhelmed or overwhelming food.

## Specific Food & Wine Pairing Guidelines

### Red Wine Pairings

#### Light Reds (Pinot Noir, Beaujolais, Grenache)
✅ **Great With**:
- Grilled fish, especially salmon
- Light poultry dishes (chicken, turkey)
- Earthy mushroom dishes
- Charcuterie and lighter cured meats
- Lighter Italian dishes (tomato-based pastas)

❌ **Avoid**:
- Heavy cream sauces
- Very spicy cuisines
- Rich chocolate desserts

**Classic Pairing**: "Pinot Noir + mushroom risotto"

#### Medium Reds (Merlot, Barbera, Tempranillo)
✅ **Great With**:
- Beef dishes (not ultra-heavy)
- Lamb dishes
- Game birds (duck, quail)
- Hard cheeses (cheddar, manchego)
- Red sauces and tomato-based dishes

**Classic Pairing**: "Tempranillo + Spanish lamb"

#### Full Reds (Cabernet Sauvignon, Barolo, Brunello)
✅ **Great With**:
- Prime rib and premium steaks
- Roasted red meats
- Game meats (venison, wild boar)
- Hearty, meat-based stews
- Strong aged cheeses

❌ **Avoid**:
- Delicate fish
- Light vegetables
- Acidic or very sweet foods

**Classic Pairing**: "Cabernet Sauvignon + grilled ribeye"

### White Wine Pairings

#### Crisp & Acidic Whites (Sauvignon Blanc, Albariño, Vermentino)
✅ **Great With**:
- Oysters and shellfish
- Light salads
- Grilled or steamed fish
- Herb-forward cuisines (Italian, Spanish)
- Goat cheese
- Vegetarian dishes with herbs

**Classic Pairing**: "Sauvignon Blanc + oysters"

#### Medium Whites (Pinot Grigio, Grüner Veltliner)
✅ **Great With**:
- Pasta with light sauces
- Roasted poultry
- Mild cheeses (mozzarella, feta)
- Asian cuisines (lighter dishes)
- Seafood pasta

**Classic Pairing**: "Pinot Grigio + pasta primavera"

#### Full & Rich Whites (Chardonnay, Viognier, Albariño aged)
✅ **Great With**:
- Cream sauces and butter dishes
- Richer fish (halibut, sea bass)
- Lobster and crab
- Roasted poultry with rich sauces
- Smoked fish
- Hard cheeses

**Classic Pairing**: "Oak-aged Chardonnay + lobster"

### Sparkling Wine Pairings

✅ **Great With**:
- Oysters and shellfish
- Appetizers and canapés
- Salty snacks
- Light desserts (fruit-based)
- Most cuisines (versatile)
- Spicy foods (acidity balances heat)

**Classic Pairing**: "Champagne + oysters"

### Sweet Wine Pairings

#### Off-Dry Wines (Riesling, Gewürztraminer)
✅ **Great With**:
- Spicy cuisines (Asian, Indian)
- Pâté and foie gras
- Blue cheese
- Fatty fish (salmon)

#### Dessert Wines (Port, Sauternes, Moscato)
✅ **Great With**:
- Chocolate desserts (especially for Port)
- Fruit-based desserts (for Moscato)
- Desserts with nuts
- Caramel and toffee flavors (for Port)

❌ **Avoid**:
- Very acidic desserts
- Overly spicy components

**Classic Pairing**: "Vintage Port + dark chocolate cake"

## Rosé Wine Pairing Guidelines

✅ **Great With**:
- Mediterranean cuisines (Greek, Italian, Spanish)
- Grilled vegetables
- Light fish
- Charcuterie
- Asian cuisines (moderate spice)
- Strawberry desserts

**Why It Works**: Rosé's lighter tannins and fruity nature provide versatility.

**Classic Pairing**: "Provençal Rosé + grilled salmon"

## Cuisine-Specific Pairing Guidelines

### Mediterranean (Italian, Spanish, Greek)
- Light reds: Vermentino, Barbera, Greek reds
- Acidic whites: Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc
- Rosé: Provence, Spanish rosés
- **Key**: Match wine's acidity and lightness to food's freshness

### Asian (Thai, Vietnamese, Chinese)
- Acidic whites: Sauvignon Blanc, Riesling
- Off-dry wines: Gewürztraminer, Riesling
- Light reds: Pinot Noir, Beaujolais
- Sparkling: Champagne, Prosecco
- **Key**: Acidity and slight sweetness balance spice and rich ingredients

### French (Classic)
- Regional matching: Burgundy wine with Burgundy food
- Loire: Crisp wines with Loire valley dishes
- Bordeaux: Full-bodied wines with meat-focused cuisine
- **Key**: Trust regional traditions—they evolved together

### Spanish
- Tempranillo-based wines with Spanish meats
- Albariño with seafood
- Sherry with tapas and charcuterie
- **Key**: Alignment between wine region and food tradition

### Indian
- Off-dry Riesling: Balances spice and richness
- Albariño: Light acidity matches complexity
- Sparkling: Cuts through heavy spices
- **Key**: Acidity and sweetness are essential

## Advanced Pairing Techniques

### 1. Tannin Bridge Pairing
Use wine tannins to enhance food protein:
- Red wine tannins bind with meat proteins
- Creates harmony and full mouthfeel
- Examples: Cabernet with steak, Barolo with lamb

### 2. Acid Bridge Pairing
Use wine acidity to brighten dish:
- Acidic wine cuts through fatty ingredients
- Cleanses palate between bites
- Examples: Sauvignon Blanc with buttery fish, Barbera with cream pasta

### 3. Fruit Bridge Pairing
Match wine fruit to sauce or ingredient:
- Red fruit wine with cherry sauce
- Stone fruit wine with peach-based dish
- Examples: Pinot Noir with berry reduction, Riesling with apricot sauce

### 4. Texture Pairing
Match wine mouthfeel to dish texture:
- Silky wine with smooth, creamy dishes
- Crisp wine with crunchy textures
- Full wine with substantial dishes

## Price Point & Accessibility Considerations

### For Pairing Recommendations

Always provide alternatives at different price points:

❌ **Poor**: "Pair with 2015 Domaine de la Romanée-Conti Pinot Noir"
- Extremely expensive, unavailable, gatekeeping

✅ **Good**: "Pair with light Pinot Noir:
- Premium: 2015 Domaine de la Romanée-Conti
- Excellent: Oregon Pinot Noir ($25-40)
- Budget-friendly: California Pinot Noir ($12-18)"

### Regional Flexibility

Expand pairing options geographically:

❌ **Rigid**: "This dish requires Burgundy"
✅ **Flexible**: "This dish pairs well with light Pinot Noir-style wines:
- French: Burgundy
- New World: Oregon, California
- Alternative: Cooler climate reds from anywhere"

## Common Pairing Mistakes

### Mistake #1: Assuming Wine Regional Rule
- ❌ "Burgundy requires Burgundy wine"
- ✅ "Burgundy pairs well with light Pinot Noir from anywhere"

### Mistake #2: Ignoring Weight Matching
- ❌ Pairing light wine with heavy dish or vice versa
- ✅ Considering overall balance and intensity

### Mistake #3: Forgetting About Sauce
- ❌ Pairing based only on protein
- ✅ Considering how sauce affects pairing

### Mistake #4: Gatekeeping with Expensive Recommendations
- ❌ Only recommending expensive wines
- ✅ Offering range of options at different budgets

### Mistake #5: Ignoring Personal Preference
- ❌ Strict pairing rules regardless of taste
- ✅ "The best pairing is what you enjoy"

## Tasting for Pairing Potential

When evaluating a wine for pairing:

1. **Identify Key Characteristics**:
   - Weight: Light, medium, full
   - Acidity: Crisp, balanced, low
   - Tannins: (reds) fine, structured, astringent
   - Sweetness: Bone-dry to sweet

2. **Consider Food Interaction**:
   - Will acidity cut through richness?
   - Will tannins enhance protein?
   - Will alcohol balance spice?
   - Will fruit notes complement sauce?

3. **Offer Flexibility**:
   - Primary pairing (ideal match)
   - Secondary options (good alternatives)
   - Price point variations
   - Regional alternatives

4. **Explain the Rationale**:
   - Not just "pairs well"
   - Explain mechanism: "Acidity cuts through rich butter..."
   - Help customer understand principles for future pairing
